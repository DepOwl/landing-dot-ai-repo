Under the Hood

Seven layers between transcript and certification.

A deposition transcript is not ordinary text. DepOwl doesn’t treat it like ordinary text. Every transcript moves through seven purpose-built layers of verification, each focused on a different way the record can go wrong. Structure, language, legal context, consistency, and uncertainty are examined separately, then brought together in one traceable errata report built for fast human review.

Layer 01

Transcript Intelligence

Before DepOwl evaluates the words, it understands the document around them: page and line structure, Q&A, speaker designations, appearances, indices, exhibits, and the formatting conventions unique to the record.

Structure mapping Format awareness Record anatomy
Layer 02

Grounded Verification

The transcript is the authority. Every finding is tied back to language that actually appears in the file, with its location preserved for review. DepOwl does not invent testimony to make a sentence work.

Verbatim grounding Page + line Traceable findings
Layer 03

Multi-Pass Proofing

Different mistakes hide in different ways. DepOwl uses specialized passes to surface grammar errors, dropped or repeated wording, malformed sentences, and real-word substitution errors (RWSE) that ordinary spellcheck can't pick up.

Multiple detection passes RWSE detection High-signal review
Layer 04

Legal Transcript Awareness

Q&A, objections, colloquy, speaker labels, exhibits, and spoken testimony do not behave like ordinary prose. DepOwl evaluates language in the context of the legal record instead of applying generic writing rules blindly.

Court-reporting conventions Q&A context Exhibit awareness
Layer 05

Record-Wide Consistency

Some of the most important errors look perfectly reasonable on their own. DepOwl looks across the transcript for details that stop agreeing, like names, dates, titles, pronouns, recurring terminology, exhibit references, and index entries.

Cross-record checks Index auditing Consistency detection
Layer 06

Calibrated Judgment

Not everything unusual is wrong. DepOwl separates high-confidence corrections from items that simply deserve another look. When testimony is ambiguous or speech patterns make the intended wording uncertain, it flags rather than rewrites.

Corrections vs. flags Confidence scoring Uncertainty preserved

Errata Assembly

DepOwl packages findings into a clean, client-ready set: corrections, flags, and specialty categories, designed for quick review and easy communication.

Security

Built to protect the record.

The browser can ask. The server decides. A legal transcript can contain names, medical information, financial records, trade secrets, testimony, and details that were never meant for a public system. DepOwl treats every uploaded transcript accordingly. Files are kept private, access is limited to the authorized job, and critical decisions are verified behind the interface, not simply trusted because a browser requested them.

Private by Default

Transcripts and deliverables live in private storage, not a public bucket or public URL, that keeps working long after you've forgotten you sent it. Every retrieval is authenticated and matched against the customer and job that own the material.

Every Matter Has a Boundary

Each job gets its own server-generated path and its own processing context. The browser doesn't choose where a file lands, and an existing file doesn't get quietly replaced. Two matters with nearly identical captions can't collapse into each other.

Trust Is Earned Server-Side

Browsers can be manipulated. DepOwl therefore revalidates important information on the backend, including job ownership, file identity, processing state, and other sensitive actions, before allowing them to proceed.

The Smallest Key That Works

Storage permissions, database roles, and backend functions get the narrowest authority that lets them do their one job, and nothing adjacent. As DepOwl grows, those boundaries are frequently reviewed alongside the features that test them.
